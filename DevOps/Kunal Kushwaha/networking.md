# Networking

## How it all started?

The internet, as we know it today, has its roots in the 1960s when the U.S. Department of Defense developed a project called ARPANET (Advanced Research Projects Agency Network). The goal was to create a communication system that could withstand potential disruptions, such as those caused by nuclear attacks. ARPANET allowed multiple computers to communicate with each other using a packet-switching technique, which laid the foundation for the modern internet.

Network between UCLA, Stanford, UC Santa Barbara, and the University of Utah was established in 1969, marking the first successful communication between computers over a network. This network eventually evolved into the internet we use today.

Concept of circuit switching and packet switching were developed to enable efficient communication between computers. Circuit switching involves establishing a dedicated communication path between two devices for the duration of the communication, while packet switching breaks data into smaller packets that are sent independently over the network and reassembled at the destination.

First website: The first website was created by Tim Berners-Lee in 1991. It was a simple page that explained the World Wide Web project and how to create web pages. The website was hosted on a NeXT computer at CERN (European Organization for Nuclear Research) and is still accessible today. You can visit it at: http://info.cern.ch/hypertext/WWW/TheProject.html

Internet Society: The Internet Society is a global nonprofit organization that promotes the open development, evolution, and use of the internet for the benefit of all people. It was founded in 1992 and works to ensure that the internet remains a global resource that is open and accessible to everyone. Anyone can submit a proposal to the Internet Society, and they have a process for reviewing and approving proposals that align with their mission and goals. Website: https://www.internetsociety.org/

## Glossary

- **internet**: A global network of interconnected computers and servers that communicate using standardized protocols.
- **protocol**: A set of rules and conventions that govern how data is transmitted and received over a network.
